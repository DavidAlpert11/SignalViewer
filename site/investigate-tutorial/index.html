    Tutorial: find out what went wrong¶

    Ten minutes with the Investigate tools on two runs made for it, with a known answer.

    The welcome screen → find out what went wrong (Investigate) opens them (or open examples/investigate/heavy_payload.csv and light_payload.csv). The same quadcopter mission flown twice through three wind gusts: with a 2.4 kg payload and with 1.2 kg. In the heavy run the roll leaves its set point three times; in the light one it holds. Why?

    (The files were built so the answer is known - see the end.)

    1. See it¶

    1. Look at the strip above the plots: the run's four phases and three red flags - Motor3 flat at its limit, in AUTO. Hover one; the light run (pick it in the strip) has none. Put Roll and DesRoll of the heavy run in a plot. Three bumps, around 38, 63 and 91 s.
    2. Right-click Mode in the signal list → Lane on every plot of this tab: the flight mode as a coloured band lined up with the tab's plots. Hover the band: the mode there and its stretch. All three bumps are in AUTO.

    2. Name it¶

    1. Press d (two cursors) and put them around the first bump (about 38.4 – 39.3 s).
    2. On the bar above the span: Find similar. The Investigate panel opens with a condition already written from your selection, on the signal most unusual inside it (whichever plot is active): Roll > 9.86 or so. Write it plainly instead: abs(Roll - DesRoll) > 5, name it Roll error. The count shows while you type: 3 stretches (all in the heavy run - hover the count: 0 in the light one).
    3. Save. The stretches are shaded on the plots. Click the name, or press ] / [, to go from one to the next.

    3. Look at all of them¶

    1. Under the list: Stretches of Roll error - a table, one row per stretch. Pick Roll, max: about the same each time.
    2. Together in a new tab: the three stretches of the signal on top of each other, lined up at their start. Switch to Motor3 in the table first, then press it again: motor 3 sits at 2000 (its limit) in every one.
    3. What differs inside: Roll, Vibe_Z, Motor3 and Wind are far from their usual level inside the stretches. Different, not yet cause.

    4. What came first¶

    1. Click the first stretch's row (both cursors on it), then What changed? on the bar.
    2. It looks at All signals of heavy_payload.csv - the file of the event, not only what is on the plots. The order: Wind (about −0.44 s), Vibe_Z (−0.3 s), Motor3 (−0.22 s), Motor1, Roll (−0.02 s). The dashed lines on the plots are those moments. (Battery_V moves with the motors' current.) Under each signal: the light run at the same time. Wind, Vibe_Z and Motor3 rise there too; Roll: no change. So the gust and the motor's answer are not enough on their own - step 12 shows what is different. On a log with many signals: type in the filter, or take a row or a whole group out with its × (the dashed lines go with it).
    3. Tick Motor3 and Roll → Lag between the two picked: Roll follows Motor3 by about 120 ms. Show aligned shifts Roll on the active plot so the two shapes meet.

    5. Check it against the other run¶

    1. Open the light run's Motor3 next to the heavy one's: the same gusts, but motor 3 stays below 2000 - it has headroom. Motor3 > 1990 as a condition: 3 stretches heavy, 0 light.
    2. Runs × conditions (under the conditions): a row per run - Payload_kg (2.4 / 1.2, found by itself: it holds one value the whole run) - and a column per condition: Roll error 3 / 0, Motor3 saturated 3 / 0. Click the 3: the heavy run at its first stretch.

    5½. Errors and the next flight¶

    • Two lines in a plot (a flight computer's X beside the true X; One into each plot in the ▾ beside Plot them puts a set of them in): ⋯ → Error in every plot with two lines opens their errors with RMS, the largest error and when, and the flight mode where it is worst when one stands out.
    • Save a template while investigating: it keeps your conditions. Open it on the next flight's file and Runs × conditions counts them there straight away.

    6. Write it up¶

    1. In the Inspector, give your notes a status: the saturation Fault, a guess that did not hold (say "the battery sags first") Rejected hypothesis. Each keeps the view it was found in (⌖).
    2. Investigate panel → Steps so far: what you did, in order. Untick what the report does not need.
    3. Report panel → Include the investigation → Preview: findings with their plots, the rejected hypothesis, the steps, the conditions per run, and how to reproduce it - then your plots.

    The answer: the gust needs more thrust from motor 3; with the heavy payload it is already near its limit, saturates, and the roll cannot be held - about 0.1–0.15 s later. The light payload leaves room, so the same gusts do nothing. What the tools gave: where (the condition), how often (3 of 3 gusts, heavy only), what was different (motor 3 at its limit), and in what order (wind → vibration → saturation → roll).

    How the files were made¶

    python tools/make_investigation_demo.py writes them again. It builds each gust in this order: wind, then vibration 0.1 s later, then motor 3 saturating 0.1 s after that (heavy only), and the roll answering what the motor could not give 0.15 s later. Nothing else in the files is related to the roll error.
