    Validation¶

    Every number Signal Viewer computes for you – a spectrum, a filter, a derivative, a mean, a comparison, a coordinate conversion – is checked against a reference that does not come from the app: a closed-form result, a definition (WGS84) or an independent textbook computation.

    The same cases run in the test suite on every change, and you can run them yourself on your own machine and installation:

    signalviewer-cli validate                  # a line per case; exit code 0 = all within tolerance
signalviewer-cli validate --junit v.xml    # the same as JUnit XML, for a qualification record

    Notes on the conventions¶

    • FFT shows the single-sided amplitude: a sine of amplitude A reads A when its frequency is on a frequency bin. Between bins the Hann window reads up to 1.4 dB (15 %) low (scalloping) – use Welch or a longer window when the exact amplitude of an off-bin tone matters. Signals with gaps are put on an even time grid first (V03); amplitudes then read low by about the share of time the gaps cover (Known limitations).
    • Welch is a one-sided power spectral density in unit²/Hz: integrating it over frequency gives the variance (V05).
    • Low- / high-pass filters are 4th-order Butterworth run forward and backward (zero phase, V09); the cut-off is the −3 dB point of the result (V06, V07). To reproduce a result in MATLAB or SciPy: filtfilt(butter(4, fd/(fs/2)), x) with fd ≈ 1.12 × the cut-off for a low-pass and 0.90 × for a high-pass (exactly: svcore.ops.zero_phase_design_cutoff). A plain filtfilt(butter(4, fc/(fs/2)), x) is at −6 dB at fc.
    • Mean, RMS, std: on evenly spaced samples, the plain sample statistics (std: population, as NumPy's default; MATLAB's std divides by N − 1). On uneven sampling they are weighted by time (V13), marked ⏱ in the Inspector.
    • Attitude: Euler angles in aerospace order (yaw, then pitch, then roll), body → NED as ArduPilot and PX4 log it (V22, V23).
