    Offline map and 3D view¶

    The network¶

    The map's default background, Offline, is the offline map the app draws from data it ships with: nothing is sent. The online backgrounds - marked ☁ in the Map background picker in the corner of every map (streets: OpenStreetMap or Esri; light / dark grey, satellite and Satellite + roads: Esri; topographic: OpenTopoMap) - are used only when you choose one (opening the picker downloads nothing: its pictures come from the tiles already on the disk); the app then requests the tiles of the area on screen from that provider - through its own server, so the page itself never talks to the internet - and keeps them in ~/.signalviewer/tiles. Such a request carries the tile's coordinates (roughly where you are looking) and the app's name. While online tiles are in use the plot shows an amber Online map sign.

    Never use the network (Settings), or SV_OFFLINE=1 in the environment (it locks the setting on, for a whole installation): the online maps are greyed out in the picker with the reason, a session that chose one shows the offline map, and the server refuses every download (app/tiles.py checks it before any request) - tiles already on the disk are still shown. A map server of your own (My maps) on this PC or a private (local-network) address is not the internet and stays allowed; one on the internet is blocked. Nothing else in the app uses the network: the 3D view's terrain and imagery are the bundled ones (no Cesium ion), and there is no update check or telemetry.

    Offline map data¶

    backend/app/basemap_data/ (17 MB) is built by python tools/make_basemap.py --all on a machine with internet (needs pyshp and osmium besides the app's libraries) and ships with the app: elevation grids (world ~11 km, Israel and surroundings ~500 m, Israel ~130 m) from the AWS "Terrain Tiles" (Mapzen / Tilezen: SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1), Natural Earth (public domain), and OpenStreetMap from Geofabrik extracts (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The app draws map tiles from them (backend/app/basemap.py, cached in ~/.signalviewer/tiles); the map shows the attribution. To refresh the data, run the tool again.

    More detailed areas (packs). Any area can get the same detail as Israel: elevation (~130 m), OpenStreetMap roads down to regional ones, places down to villages, airports and runways:

    python tools/make_basemap.py --pack cyprus --bbox 34.5,32.2,35.75,34.65 --countries europe/cyprus

    --bbox is south,west,north,east; --countries the Geofabrik extracts covering it (comma-separated); --zoom 9 or 8 for coarser (smaller) elevation. The pack is written to backend/app/basemap_data/packs/<name>/ (pack.json, dem.npz, vectors.json.gz; ~1.4 MB for Cyprus) and the app uses every pack in that folder - copy a pack folder there on an offline machine.

    Country borders. The offline map and the satellite photos get the borders and the country names from the app itself (Natural Earth 1:50m, simplified to about 3 km - good for seeing which country, not for a border's exact line): Borders, in the Map background picker. The street maps draw their own.

    My maps and keeping an area offline (1.28)¶

    My maps (Map picker → Add a map…): a raster MBTiles file (read on this PC, fully offline) or a tile server of your own (XYZ, or TMS with its y axis flipped - for example a server on the local network). It is a background like any other, in 2D, 3D, exported pictures and offline map pages. The list lives in ~/.signalviewer/mymaps.json; Remove from My maps forgets one (the file or server is not touched).

    Keep this area offline… (the same picker): before going to the field, download the tiles of the area on screen at the zooms you choose. The number of tiles and the size are estimated first; the download runs in the background with progress and Stop; the kept areas are listed with their size and Delete (a tile another kept area also covers stays). Only where the map's terms allow bulk downloads: the ground heights (open data) and your own servers. OpenStreetMap, Esri and OpenTopoMap forbid it - the dialog says so; the places you look at are still kept in the cache as before. The list lives in ~/.signalviewer/kept_areas.json.

    Height profile and arrows along the path (1.28)¶

    Height profile (Profile in the map's bar): under the map, the object's height and the ground under it along the distance flown, for any recording with positions (/api/profile, the ground from the same heights as the 3D terrain). The cursor's moment is marked; a click in the profile moves the cursor. Where the ground data is coarse (outside the detailed areas) the profile says so and how to get finer ground: .hgt files (Settings → Map and objects, the folder of your elevation files) or Keep this area offline….

    Arrows along the path (an object's settings): any two signals that are north and east components - a wind, a velocity, a force - drawn as arrows along the object's track, on the 2D map and in 3D. The file's own pair is offered by itself (an ArduPilot log's estimated wind, a CSV's north / east velocity).

    3D view¶

    CesiumJS (pinned, frontend/package.json) is loaded only when a plot is switched to 3D; its web workers and data files are copied into the UI build under /cesium/ (frontend/vite.config.ts), nothing comes from the internet. Terrain heights come from /api/terrain/{z}/{x}/{y} (the same bundled elevation grids as the offline map: sea at 0 m, the Jordan rift really below it). The object models are built by python tools/make_models.py into frontend/public/models/. Graphics: with a graphics card Cesium draws at the display's frame rate; drawn in software (virtual machines, remote desktop) it is slow - the app then uses one sample per pixel and less terrain detail (about 3x faster) and shows a note.

    Outside the app (1.26, backend/app/scene3d.py): the 3D view in reports, the replay page and its own offline HTML file (its ⋯ menu; signalviewer-cli map --3d, sv.scene_html / sv.scene_image). CesiumJS cannot go into one file, so the page draws the same data with three.js r159 (MIT, embedded): the offline map draped on the bundled terrain (97 × 97 heights over the tracks' box and a margin), each track drawn as the app draws it (a fixed width on screen; a navigation solution dashed, a receiver as points), the app's models with heading, pitch and roll, heights ×1 / ×2 / ×5. The 2D map's page and the 3D page share one toolbar and one relation box (report_assets/relation.js, mappage.css, the words in mapimage.PAGE_STRINGS): the camera Free · Around it · Behind it on a chosen object (Behind it: behind it, along its heading), + / −, Fit, Trail (whole / 60 s / 20 s), Relation (show / hide), light / dark; an attitude indicator in 3D; the address keeps the view (#t=…&cam=…&obj=…); Closest in moves the time (inside a report: the report's time, replay-seek); the box is the app's (range, horizontal, Δalt, bearing, relative bearing, closure, closest-in, heading Δ) with the pair's rings and a dashed line with the distance. The objects are read as the Tacview export reads them. Where the browser has no WebGL the page shows the scene's picture (matplotlib, the same picture as in PDF / Word / PNG). The scene is local east / north / up metres around the tracks' middle (a flat earth: fine for tracks of kilometres, not for hundreds of them).

    What is a position (P2, 1.26)¶

    One rule, in track_of (backend/app/routes/analysis.py, no_position_as_gap), used by every drawing and export: a sample whose latitude and longitude are both exactly 0, or outside ±90 / ±180, or not finite, is not a position - it is a gap. Receivers and autopilots write 0, 0 for "no fix" (ArduPilot's EAHR without an external AHRS: 0, 0 for the whole flight; a GPS before its first fix). Detection still goes by names; Add all and the found list then ask /api/object_positions and leave out an object with no position (the message names it), and note one with under 2 % positions. An object already in a saved session that turns out empty is drawn as nothing, left out of Fit and of every export, and its chip says no position. A latitude of 0 alone (a place on the equator) is a position.

    The ground's heights (P5, 1.26)¶

    basemap.ground_at and terrain_tile take the finest of: a bundled fine grid (the packs: Israel at 139 m) → the user's elevation files (SRTM .hgt, 1201² = 3" or 3601² = 1", in ~/.signalviewer/terrain/ or the folder chosen in Settings; read with numpy) → terrarium tiles (Mapzen / AWS open data: SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1; level 12, ~30 m), fetched only when the network is allowed (not Never use the network, no SV_OFFLINE) and kept in the tile cache with the same rules as online map tiles → the bundled world grid (11 km). A fetch runs in the background: the answer comes from what is there, and /api/terrain/version changes when finer heights arrived (the app's 3D view then asks its terrain again; the tile URLs carry the version so the browser's cache does not keep the coarse one). basemap.step_at(lat, lon) is the step of the best source at a point, never fetching.

    The offline 3D page says what its ground is: the footer always ("terrain: 139 m grid"), and a line over the scene when the step at its centre is over 1 km. Its ground reaches the horizon: the near mesh (the tracks' box, 129 × 129) over a far one (8 × its side, 40 to 200 km, 129 × 129, 2 m lower and not drawn inside the near box), fog from 0.6 × to 1.4 × the far side, a sky gradient, the sun 35° high from the north-west. Heights ×1 by default (honest), ×2 / ×5 / ×10; "flat here" beside the choice when the relief is under 4 % of the near side. The PDF / Word / PNG picture draws the far ground as far as twice the near box. The page is a flat local frame: Earth's curvature is not drawn (at 100 km it would drop the horizon by ~800 m).

    Hiding an object (P6, 1.26)¶

    One rule: a hidden object (MapObject.hidden) is not drawn, not part of Fit, and keeps its place in the list (greyed, struck through). shownObjects(plot) (mapObjects.ts) is what the drawing loops of Globe3D.tsx and GeoChart.tsx iterate (their track fetch, models, markers, labels, tracks, Fit); lookups of another object (a borrowed attitude, the pair) use the whole list. A hidden one of the pair shows "— hidden —" in the relation box; a camera on it goes to Free. Pictures skip it; the offline pages start with it hidden and their legend rows are buttons (svLegend in report_assets/relation.js; click, Alt+click = only this one). Exports (Tacview, CSV, KML) keep every object.

    The Map is 3D (P4, 1.26)¶

    One Map, and it is the 3D view; "2D" is its camera From above. Saved values do not change: mode: "geo" with objects is drawn by the 3D view (isMapPlot, mapObjects.ts) with the camera From above (Plot.camera absent on such a plot = "top"; mapCamera); mode: "3d" as before (absent = Free, or Around it with a followed object); new maps are "3d". Free writes camera: "free". 1.28 renamed the cameras on screen only - Free ("free"), From above ("top"), Around it (absent with a followed object), Behind it ("chase"), Cockpit ("cockpit") - the saved values are unchanged, so a session opens with the same camera as before. A 1.25 build opening a session from this one draws its maps as before. mode: "geo" without objects - a lon / lat X-Y on a map - is unchanged (GeoChart).

    When 3D cannot draw (hasWebGL() false - webgl.ts, no CesiumJS loaded for the check - or the context lost) the Map is drawn by GeoChart with "Shown flat – this computer cannot draw 3D" and the same bar. Globe3DFallback.tsx (tracks on bare axes) is gone. ui.mapFlat forces the flat drawing (P8's Map drawing: Always flat; the flat-map e2e tests use it implicitly - their browser has no WebGL).

    Reports and pages: a Map goes out as the 3D view on its camera (plotSpec → mode: "3d", scene.camera); From above → the flat map picture (report._map_picture → mapimage.render), any other camera → scene3d.render; the live page opens on that camera (D.camera). The flat page (map.html) inside the 3D page's frame for a browser without WebGL is not done (the page shows its picture there, as before).

    Places (P7, 1.26)¶

    Named points, lines and areas on every Map, and saved camera views. Two lists: Places of this session (Workspace.places, saved with the session and with a template made from it) and My places (the user's own, in every session - the app keeps them in ~/.signalviewer/places.json, a file that can be given to a colleague). One panel holds them (the Map's ⋯ → Places…); the command palette has Go to a place… and Add a place….

    • Positions are read by one parser (svcore/places.py parse_coords): decimal degrees ("32.687, 35.39") or degrees-minutes-seconds (32°41'13"N 35°23'24"E, N32 41 13 E35 23 24); without hemisphere letters the order is latitude, longitude. A new point's height is the ground there (/api/ground).
    • Import: .kml (points, lines, polygons - their outer ring), .kmz (the zip's KML), .geojson (Point / LineString / Polygon and their Multi- forms), .csv (name, lat, lon[, alt], a header optional; degrees-minutes-seconds in the cells work). A shape with no valid position is left out and counted.
    • Drawing: in 3D a point is its icon on a thin 30 m pole with its name; lines and areas are draped on the ground (Cesium ground primitives) - in the software-WebGL light mode, for the frame rate, plain lines 2 m over the ground under each vertex. The flat drawing, both offline pages and the pictures draw the same shapes. Places take part in Fit only when the Map has no objects.
    • Readout and relation: the mouse readout adds the nearest shown place within 50 km; the relation box's Compared with list ends with the places (Plot.relPlace; a place is a fixed point).
    • Exports: the KML file holds every shown place (a saved view as a LookAt); the ACMI file holds the points as stationary objects - Type=Navaid+Static+Waypoint, an airfield Ground+Static+Aerodrome (both from the ACMI 2.2 type list).

    Settings > Map and objects (P8, 1.26)¶

    One module reads and writes these settings (frontend/src/mapDefaults.ts, keys sv3.map.*). They apply to new things only - a new object, a new Map, the next export; an open session, a template or an object already made is never changed by them. Drawn things (names, the track's width, units, coordinates) follow the setting at once, in the app and in the pages and pictures made after.

    Setting Choices (default first)
    Icon of a new object, per class (truth / navigation / receiver / other) aircraft · uav · drone · missile · ship · vehicle
    Camera of a new Map Free · From above · Around it · Behind it
    Map drawing Automatic · Always flat (light)
    Relation box on a new Map / Names on the Map shown · hidden
    Track width 2.5 px · 1.5 · 4
    Model size of a new object medium (26) · small (18) · large (36)
    Distances and heights m / km · ft / NM (below 1 NM in feet)
    Coordinates decimal degrees · degrees-minutes-seconds · MGRS (80° S to 84° N; the polar grid is not done)
    Elevation files a folder of SRTM .hgt files (empty: ~/.signalviewer/terrain)
    Export rate for Tacview / KML 10 · 5 · 2 · 1 samples a second
    Story on · off; about 90 s · 45 s · 3 min

    The wow (P9, 1.26)¶

    • Light: the sun where it was - the recording's own time when its times are Unix dates, else 10:00 local solar time at an equinox (scene3d.sun_position, NOAA's low-precision formulas, ~0.5°). The app: sky atmosphere, lighting, fog; soft model shadows only while the camera is on an object.
    • In space: a drop line and a dark disc under each object; its last 10 s brighter over the whole track.
    • Flights: every camera change eases there over ~1.2 s (none with the system's reduce motion).
    • Cockpit: a fifth camera at the object, along its heading and pitch, rolling with it, 70°; its own model (and any within 20 m) hidden. The flat drawing treats it as Behind it.
    • Flight display (Display in the bar; on by default Behind it and in Cockpit) and a mini-map while the camera is near an object (a click flies there).
    • The error, drawn: when the relation box's pair is one vehicle (median distance under 20 m) a see-through surface between the tracks, green → amber → red at the distance's 95th percentile, with its scale and a toggle in the relation box. The app draws it while the camera is within 3 km (from further it is under a pixel); the flat drawing colours the compared track instead.
    • Light mode (software WebGL): sky, shadows, drop lines, the 10 s trail and the error surface are off; the display, mini-map, cockpit and flights stay (no GPU cost).

    Story mode (P10, 1.26)¶

    svcore/story.py picks the moments: the page's marks (the log's errors and warnings, health findings, the user's notes) and, from the reference object of the tab's first Map, takeoff / landing (the height above the ground crossing 5 m for 3 s), the highest and fastest points, the steepest bank (over 30°), passing within 200 m of a place, and for the pair the largest error (one vehicle) or the closest and farthest moments. A moment within 3 s of a stronger one is dropped; at most 12. The live page's player runs the time so the recording takes about the Settings' length (×1 to ×60), at ×1 from 3 s before a moment to 2 s after, and stops 4 s on it; the map frames fly to its object (Around it ~400 m; Behind it for a bank, an error, a takeoff or a landing) and give the reader's camera back at the end.
